## <a id="g-crosswalk"></a>The Stripe–Adyen Vocabulary Crosswalk: How to Read It

The definitions above are in the book's own words. Each provider's documentation uses its own, and **each vocabulary follows that provider's object model**. Stripe exposes a seller as one account; Adyen separates the legal entity, the account holder, the balance account and the transfer instrument. Adyen calls a capture, a cancel and a refund modifications, each a separate asynchronous request with its own reference; Stripe has no word for the group. Stripe speaks of disputes for cards and bank debits alike where Adyen speaks of chargebacks, unpaid direct debits included. The nine tables that follow set such differences side by side, one area at a time.

Read a row from the left. **Concept** is the book's neutral name. The next two columns give the word each provider's documentation uses, with field names and statuses in code type. **Note** says how far the translation holds: “Same function” where the two words can be swapped; “Divided differently” or “No one-to-one match” where one provider splits what the other keeps together; **false friend** where the same word means different things; and “__NONE__” where the owning chapter searched for a counterpart and found none. That last phrase is a dated search result and not proof of absence. The final column links to the chapter that treats the concept and cites the documentation behind both cells.

The pairing is this book's mapping and therefore an inference: **neither company publishes a translation to the other**. A row was included only if both provider terms appear with that meaning in a chapter of the book, or were confirmed on the provider's documentation page as fetched on 17 November 2026; the __XDOC__ rows of the second kind are marked “documentation” in the last column and their pages are listed under Sources and Evidence. Pairs that could not be confirmed either way were left out. A team that integrates both providers, as Larkfield does, should define its own neutral terms from these tables and map each provider's words onto them, instead of letting whichever provider it integrated first name its database columns and its states.
