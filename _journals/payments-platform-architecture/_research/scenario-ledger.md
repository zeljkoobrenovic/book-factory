# Larkfield — Canonical Scenario Ledger

Larkfield is **fictional**. Every figure, system, name and event below is invented for teaching. Chapters must agree with this ledger. If a chapter needs a new fact, append it under "Additions by chapter" rather than inventing a conflicting one. Never present Larkfield as a real company.

Larkfield is a composite of patterns common among software platforms that embed payments. It is **not** a disguised version of any real company. It uses Stripe and Adyen by name because the book compares the two; everything Larkfield does with them is illustration, and nothing here is a statement about how either company treats a real customer. Larkfield's own systems are named by role ("the payment service", "the ledger", "the booking app", "the data warehouse").

## The company

- **Larkfield B.V.** — a software platform for independent fitness and wellness studios (yoga, pilates, climbing, martial arts, boutique gyms), headquartered in **Rotterdam, the Netherlands**. Founded **2017** by **Sofie Vermeer** (CEO) and **Daniel Osei** (CTO). Privately held; Series B in 2023.
- Product: class scheduling and booking, memberships, a member app, front-desk check-in and sales, and **payments** (members pay studios through Larkfield).
- **2026 figures (fiscal year = calendar year):** **6,200 studios**; **2.9 million** active members; payment volume processed for studios **€1.12 billion** in **31.4 million payments**; Larkfield revenue **€46.0 million** (software subscriptions €24.5M, payments revenue €19.8M, other €1.7M); **340 employees**, of whom **96** in engineering and **11** in the payments team.
- Markets: Netherlands (home, 2,300 studios), Germany (1,450), United Kingdom (1,100), Belgium (650), United States (700, since 2025). Currencies: EUR, GBP, USD.

**Larkfield's payment fee to studios:** **1.9% + €0.25** per online card payment, **0.8% + €0.25** per direct debit, **€0.35** flat per iDEAL payment and **1.4% + €0.10** per in-person payment, charged as a platform fee on each payment; the provider's processing cost comes out of that fee. Payments revenue of about €19.8M (about 1.77% of volume, blended) is these fees; the providers' processing costs were **€12.6M**, leaving a payments margin of **€7.2M**.

**Payment method mix, online, 2026 (by volume):** cards 52% (of which about 70% debit), SEPA Direct Debit 21% (memberships in NL and DE), iDEAL 14% (passes and first payments in NL), Bancontact 4%, Bacs Direct Debit 5% (UK memberships), other 4% (Apple Pay and Google Pay are counted under cards).

## Why two providers

- **Stripe** since **2017**: Stripe Billing for Larkfield's own subscriptions; Stripe Connect for member payments. Today Connect carries the **United Kingdom, the United States and Belgium**, and every studio onboarded before 2024 that has not been migrated. **2026 share of member payment volume: 44% (€493M).**
- **Adyen** since **2024** (Adyen for Platforms): chosen for in-person payments on one platform with online, and for local acquiring in the Netherlands and Germany. Today Adyen carries **all front-desk terminals** and online payments for studios in the **Netherlands and Germany** onboarded or migrated since 2024. **2026 share: 56% (€627M).**
- The split is by studio, not by payment: a studio is on one provider. **1,140 studios** onboarded before 2024 in NL and DE are still on Stripe for online payments while using Adyen terminals at the front desk, which is the source of several incidents below.
- Larkfield never holds member funds: on both providers the studio's money sits in the provider's regulated balance until payout. Larkfield is not a payment institution and is not the merchant of record for class sales; the studio is the seller.

## The Keel program (2027)

**Keel** is the 2027 program that puts the payments estate on an even keel. Decisions **K1–K8** are canonical; chapters explain them, they do not change them.

- **K1 — One writer, one key.** Every money-moving call goes through the payment service, and its idempotency key is derived from the business event, never generated per network call. For a renewal the key is membership id plus billing period plus the number of the scheduled attempt: a timeout retry of the same attempt reuses the key, while the next scheduled attempt days later is a new business event with a new key.
- **K2 — The provider's record is the truth; the webhook is the doorbell.** Receivers verify, persist and acknowledge; a state change is confirmed against the provider's own record (the payment object where the API lets Larkfield read it, the settlement report where it does not); a job sweeps everything still pending.
- **K3 — A ledger entry for every movement.** Fees, splits, refunds, disputes, payouts and returns are entries; clearing accounts must return to zero; the reconciliation job covers both providers, one run per provider, started when that provider's reports are ready.
- **K4 — Card numbers never touch Larkfield.** Hosted fields and provider tokens only; network tokens for stored cards; a script inventory for the booking widget.
- **K5 — Renewals are spread and rate-limited by Larkfield, not by the provider.** The renewal job paces itself over 18 hours and retries on a schedule tied to decline codes.
- **K6 — Verification is state, not a form.** Each studio's requirements, deadlines and capability status are mirrored and surfaced in the back office, with an owner for every deadline.
- **K7 — Versions are upgraded on a calendar.** One planned API upgrade per provider per half-year, tested against recorded traffic.
- **K8 — Two providers, one rehearsed switch.** The payment service can move a studio's online card payments to the other provider; the switch is rehearsed quarterly for the 50 largest studios; no orchestration product is added.

## Recurring worked examples (amounts and dates are canonical)

- **Mila's class pass.** On **Tuesday 9 March 2027 at 19:42** Mila Janssen buys a **ten-class pass for €120.00** from Studio Noord in the member app, paying with a **Dutch Visa debit card** held in her phone's wallet; the wallet authenticates her on the device (fingerprint), so there is no 3-D Secure challenge. Studio Noord is on Adyen. Larkfield's fee is 1.9% + €0.25 = **€2.53**; the studio receives **€117.47**. The illustrative provider cost inside that fee is **€0.61** *(assumption for teaching; chapter 5 breaks it down)*, leaving Larkfield **€1.92**. Authorization is approved at 19:42; the payment is captured automatically; the studio's payout that includes it is sent on **Thursday 11 March** and, in the best case, reaches the studio's bank the same day. (Larkfield's own payout record marks it `arrived` only on 25 March, when the return window chapter 14 defines has closed with no return; "arrived" as a record state is not the day the money lands.) Chapters that need the Stripe version use the same pass bought the same evening at **Ironworks Climbing's** sister example in euros only when the contract says so; otherwise they say "had Studio Noord been on Stripe".
- **Mila's membership renewal.** Mila also holds a **€49.00 monthly membership** at Studio Noord, set up on **12 January 2027** by typing in the same Visa debit card and passing a 3-D Secure challenge in her banking app (the customer-initiated first payment that later renewals refer to), renewed on the **1st of each month** as a merchant-initiated card payment. On **1 April 2027** the renewal is declined (insufficient funds); it is retried on **4 April** and succeeds. Journey 2 follows it.
- **The refund after payout.** On **18 March 2027** Mila asks for a refund of the unused part of the pass (**€84.00**, seven classes), after Studio Noord was paid on 11 March. Journey 3 follows it.
- **The €120 dispute.** A different member, **Bram de Wit**, disputes a €120.00 pass bought at Studio Noord on **2 February 2027** paid with a **Dutch Mastercard credit card**, as "not recognized"; the chargeback arrives on **24 February 2027** with a **€15.00** dispute fee *(illustrative)*. Under Larkfield's terms the studio carries a lost dispute and its fee; if the studio's balance cannot cover it, the loss falls on Larkfield as the platform. Journey 4 follows it.
- **The annual plan.** Ironworks Climbing pays **€1,890** up front on **1 February 2027** for an annual €189 plan at one location (chapter 31).
- **The first of the month.** Recurring memberships are 61% of online volume, about **€555 million** a year or **€46 million** a month, in about **940,000 renewals** a month. The Renewal Wave is the **38%** of them (about **357,000 renewals**, **€17.5 million**) attempted in the first six hours of the 1st; the rest renew on each member's own anniversary day.

## Additions by chapter

Append new canonical facts here, one line each, prefixed with the chapter folder. Append only; never rewrite others' lines.

- `12-payment-ledgers`: Before the ledger existed (until 2025), what each studio was owed was computed by a nightly query: the sum of its `paid` payments, less Larkfield's fees and the payouts already sent. The 2025 ledger was posted by a nightly job that wrote an entry when a payment became `paid` and when a payout was created; refunds made after payout, chargeback fees and currency conversion had no entries of their own until Keel (K3).
- `12-payment-ledgers`: The Unexplained €0.4 Million: at year-end 2025 the ledger's "owed to studios" balance was **€412,000 higher** than the two providers' balances. Breakdown: refunds after payout **€317,200**; chargeback fees **€73,500** (4,900 disputes at the illustrative €15.00); currency-conversion rounding in Larkfield's own posting job **€21,300** (net). 317,200 + 73,500 + 21,300 = 412,000.
- `12-payment-ledgers`: Under K3 the ledger's accounts, per provider and currency, are "funds at provider", "owed to studio" (one per studio), "fees owed to Larkfield", and three clearing accounts that must return to zero: "sales awaiting settlement report", "payouts in transit" and "to recover from studio" (one per studio). Every entry carries the provider's reference and the business event's id, and entries are never edited: a correction is a new entry.
- `03-clearing-and-settlement`: Studio Noord's Adyen account uses a sales day that closes at midnight local time and a payout delay of two business days (so Tuesday 9 March sales are paid Thursday 11 March). On Wednesday 10 March 2027 Ines Bakker asked support whether Mila's €120 had been paid; the payment record store showed `paid`, the provider balance showed it as not yet paid out, her bank showed nothing. Had the studio been on Stripe's Dutch default (3 business days from capture), the funds would have become available on Friday 12 March.
- `15-payment-reconciliation-and-reporting`: Under K3 the reconciliation job runs once per provider on that provider's report-ready signal (not at a fixed hour) and writes ledger entries only from report lines, never from webhooks. Matching keys: the payout ID on Stripe; merchant account plus batch number, and the balance platform's daily report per studio, on Adyen. Unmatched amounts are open items with an amount, an age and an owner; an item open for more than **five business days** goes to Priya Raman's weekly review.
- `13-balances-and-funds-flows`: Before Keel the studio back office showed one running total per provider, copied from the provider, with no dates or reasons; under K3 it shows each studio three amounts with dates, named by function: "on its way" (Stripe pending; Adyen bookings whose value date is in the future), "ready to pay out" (Stripe available; Adyen settled to the balance account) and "held" (Stripe reserve; Adyen deposit). Behind Mila's €120.00 on Adyen: €117.47 in Studio Noord's balance account, €2.53 less the €0.61 processing cost *(assumption)* = €1.92 in Larkfield's liable balance account. Neither provider holds a reserve or deposit on Studio Noord.
- `08-payment-state-machines`: Under Keel the payment record store keeps its five states as the summary and adds, per row: the provider's status as received, `waiting_on` (`customer`, `provider` or empty), `closed_reason` for a pending payment that ended without money (declined, canceled, expired), and child rows for attempts, captures, refunds and disputes, each with an amount and its own status. `paid` means "the provider has confirmed the capture", never "settled"; on Stripe a row moves only after the PaymentIntent is re-read, on Adyen on the webhook (automatic capture: `paid` on AUTHORISATION success). A late CAPTURE_FAILED moves a row from `paid` back to `authorized`. An unknown provider status is stored and raised for review, never mapped to a default. Femke's review of the record with Tom Hendriks is in late March 2027.
- `14-payouts-and-money-movement`: Under Keel a payout in the payment record store has four states: `instructed`, `sent` (the provider reports booked or paid), `arrived` and `returned`. A sent payout stays open for **10 business days** on both providers *(Larkfield's policy, an assumption)*; `arrived` is set by a provider confirmation of the credit (Adyen `credited` on instant routes) or by the window closing with no return. A return is a new ledger entry linked to the payout: within the window it clears "payouts in transit" back to "funds at provider" with the studio's debt still open; after the window it credits "owed to studio" again. Since the Payout That Bounced (412 payouts, average about €937), a change of a studio's bank details triggers a check of the account holder's name and holds that studio's next payout until the check passes or a person approves it. The payout carrying Mila's €117.47 is marked sent on Thursday 11 March 2027 and arrived on Thursday 25 March.
- `01-journey-of-a-payment`: Mila's class pass: the basic card dispute window is taken to close on **7 July 2027** (120 calendar days from Tuesday 9 March 2027; the author's arithmetic, since Visa counts from the transaction processing date, which may be a day later). Chapters say "about 7 July" or "early July". The acquirer of record for the pass is treated as Adyen N.V., labeled as inference wherever it is stated (Adyen lists the countries where it has its own acquiring connection but names no acquiring entity per country; ruling 27). On 10 March Ines's ticket reads "It says paid. Paid to whom?"; Aylin Demir forwards it and Femke de Graaf answers it with four questions (who approved, has money moved, when does the studio receive it, until when can it be taken back).
- `04-refunds-reversals-and-disputes`: The €120 dispute (Studio Noord, on Adyen): the booking app holds Bram de Wit's logged-in purchase session and check-ins to **two classes** with the pass, on **4 and 11 February 2027**; the payment is assumed to carry no 3-D Secure liability shift *(assumption)*; Larkfield submits the single defense on **3 March 2027** *(illustrative)*; Adyen's documented Mastercard defense deadline is 40 days from the 24 February notification (**5 April 2027**). - `19-api-versioning`: The Version Cliff in detail: until March 2026 the billing integration sent no version and used a Stripe library that carried none, so it ran on the account default `2022-11-15` (last moved early 2023).
