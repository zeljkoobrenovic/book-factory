## Notes and what could not be fully verified

1. **Stripe's 90-day figure has two values on the same site.** The status page as rendered in a headless browser on 17 November 2026 displays “99.999% uptime for the last 90 days” for the Stripe API (the string is written into the page's own script by the server). The page's data endpoint `https://www.stripestatus.com/uptime_showcase?components=2p0n66vlgnn2`, which the rendered page did not display, returned 99.94 (90 days), 99.91 (60) and 99.96 (30) for the same component that day, with partial-outage time on 22, 23 and 27 September and 14 November 2026. The chapter states only the displayed figure. `status.stripe.com` itself is client-rendered and returned no text to a plain fetch or to the headless browser.
2. **Adyen Annual Report 2025.** The official PDF is linked from `investors.adyen.com/financials/2025` (a `brand.adyen.com/api/asset/.../download` link, 144 MB, 233 pages, created 4 March 2026). The three quotes match it exactly. That PDF carries the same disclaimer as the Bundestag copy: it is a “printed version”, and the official report is the report package filed in the regulator's format. The filed package itself was not read; the source entry says so.
3. **stripe.com serves a localized site to this location** (`/us/` paths redirect to `/nl/`). English pages were read through `/en-nl/` paths and, for the 2023 letter, the US PDF. The URLs in Sources are unchanged and resolve.
4. **Stripe marketing figures not used.** A web search surfaced Stripe pages said to cite “99.999% historical uptime” (enterprise page) and “99.9995%+” (a support page). They were not fetched and are not in the chapter.
5. **Adyen's site also has an Infrastructure page** (`https://www.adyen.com/infrastructure`, n.d., fetched): “designed for maximum uptime through a redundant and stateless service-oriented architecture (SOA) that simultaneously accepts payments on multiple physical hosting locations”; “hosts its main systems in separate data centers in Europe, the United States, India, Singapore, Japan and Australia”; “All payments processed are stored in Europe and our isolated India environment.” Not added to this chapter (length); see “For other pages”.
6. **Link check.** Every URL in Sources returned HTTP 200 on 17 November 2026 (stripe.com URLs after a locale redirect). All `[[permalinks]]` are in the style guide's list.
7. **Ruling 22.** All inline external links were reduced to plain attributions; every URL was already in Sources or was added.
8. **Rulings 39, 42 and 43.** Status-page statements: Adyen's 30-day figure, its definition and its disclaimer were read from the HTML that `status.adyen.com` served on 17 November 2026; no statement is made about Adyen's incident history. Stripe's entry counts are labeled as the author's count on that date, from `www.stripestatus.com/history.json`. The chapter makes no claim that Adyen has published nothing on load testing, a peak freeze, headroom or replication. “Not public” cells were re-searched beyond the documentation indexes (site pages, legal pages, web search) and reworded as “Not found (documentation, help center, site; November 2026)”.
9. **Spec.** Paragraph-level controlling ideas and evidence in `spec.json` were updated to the corrected facts (p10, p11, p12, p20, p25, p30, p32), two open questions rewritten, changelog entry added. Intent, thesis, supporting claims and acceptance criteria (copied from the journal spec) were left as they are: “neither company publishes its failover design” still holds as worded, and “a rolling 30-day figure” is now incomplete rather than wrong.
10. **Length.** After the corrections the draft stood at 6,617 words against a limit of 6,500; the additions were tightened to 6,491. `timetoread` is now 28 min (6,491 ÷ 230).
