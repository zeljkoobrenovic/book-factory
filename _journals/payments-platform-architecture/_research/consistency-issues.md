# Consistency rulings — SETTLED

Numbered cross-chapter rulings. Writers and reviewers apply every ruling that touches their page. A ruling wins over a chapter line.

## From the journal flow rubric (17 November 2026; verdict: sound with notes)

1. **Where payment state lives (Stripe retrievable object vs Adyen result code, modifications and webhooks with no payment GET in the Checkout API)** is owned by `payment-state-machines` (ch. 8). `payment-api-design` (ch. 17) states it in one sentence with a link and spends its space on stable identifiers, normalized vocabulary, error models and the line between the response and what arrives later.
2. **Verification as continuing account state with deadlines that switch capabilities** is owned by `payment-compliance-and-regulation` (ch. 26). `platform-onboarding-and-verification` (ch. 34) starts from it as given and covers only what the platform does: request minimal capabilities, mirror requirements, give every deadline an owner, the seller's view.
3. **Acquiring membership and the acquirer of record** are owned by `acquiring-and-network-connectivity` (ch. 6); **custody of funds, safeguarding and what a license lets a provider hold** by `licences-and-safeguarding` (ch. 16). Each gives the other one sentence and a link. Chapter 6 glosses "e-money license" and "partner bank" in one clause each.
4. **"Confirm against the provider's own record"**: `idempotency-and-retries` (ch. 18) covers it for a request whose outcome is unknown; `payment-webhooks-and-events` (ch. 20) owns the receiver's contract (persist, deduplicate, confirm, sweep). Chapter 18 links to 20 instead of restating the receiver's steps.
5. **Request limits**: the limits themselves and load shedding are owned by `payment-developer-platform` (ch. 21); `payment-peak-load` (ch. 40) cites it and adds only pacing, fairness and capacity.
6. **Who holds the invoice (Stripe yes, Adyen no)** is owned by `subscriptions-and-billing-engines` (ch. 27). `invoicing-collections-and-dunning` (ch. 29) uses it in one sentence; `journey-failed-renewal` (ch. 43) leads with the hand-off, not the mechanism.
7. **A loss falls on the booked balance, and the platform funds a refund after payout** is owned by `platform-payment-models` (ch. 33) for the liability rule and `splits-balances-and-platform-payouts` (ch. 35) for the mechanics. `journey-platform-payment` (ch. 44) leads with the hand-off.
8. **Forward references in Part II.** `authorization-optimization` (ch. 9) glosses "network token" (a card-network stand-in for the card number that stays valid when the card is reissued) and "risk block" in one clause each and links to `network-tokens` and `payment-fraud-and-risk`. `payment-routing-and-orchestration` (ch. 10) glosses "provider token" and "balance" and links to `tokenization-vaulting-and-pci` and `balances-and-funds-flows`. `in-person-payments` (ch. 11) glosses "liability shift" and links to `three-d-secure-and-sca`. `payment-state-machines` (ch. 8) may use "webhook" as known vocabulary (the reader is an engineer) and links to `payment-webhooks-and-events`.
9. **Chapter 50 (`payments-what-is-moving`)** ties each change to the two bets: for agents, stablecoins and instant payments it says which promise the change moves and which of the two companies is positioned, by what it built first, to carry it. It then performs the journal's closing.
10. **Journeys (Part IX)** lead every step with the hand-off between mechanisms, and point to the owning chapter for the mechanism itself.

## From the part-spec authors (17 November 2026)

11. **Stripe's acquiring is not "partner banks everywhere".** Stripe's own entities are the acquirer in the EEA, the UK and much of Asia-Pacific; it is a payment facilitator under named partner banks in the United States, Canada, Japan, India and Thailand, and lists its own Georgia merchant acquirer limited purpose bank among its US acquirers. Adyen reports 84% of 2025 volume as full-stack and uses banking partners where it has no acquiring license. No chapter may say "Adyen acquires itself, Stripe uses partners" without these qualifications.
12. **Adyen's Checkout API has no endpoint that returns a payment by its identifier.** "Fetch the object" advice applies to Stripe; on Adyen the confirming record is the webhook plus the settlement report (ledger K2).
13. **Idempotency keys and scheduled retries.** A key derived only from membership and billing period would replay a stored decline on a later scheduled attempt (Stripe replays saved responses for at least 24 hours; Adyen keeps keys 7–14 days). The ledger's K1 includes the attempt number.
14. **Mila's class pass is paid from a phone wallet**: device authentication, no 3-D Secure challenge. The 3-D Secure challenge example is the set-up of her membership on 12 January 2027 (ledger).
15. **Terms that are not network terms.** "Soft decline" and "hard decline" are industry shorthand, not Visa or Mastercard terms; "Visa Claims Resolution" does not appear in the current Visa rule text. Say so where used.
16. **Phrases not found in sources.** "Built, not bought" is not a phrase in any fetched Adyen document; do not put it in quotation marks or attribute it. EU "deemed supplier" rules cover goods (2021) and two service sectors (from 2028), not fitness studios.
17. **Mastercard rules** were not readable directly (403); Mastercard facts come through Stripe's and Adyen's documentation and are cited that way.
18. **Pre-financing of payouts is documented for Adyen, not for Stripe.** Adyen's Sales Day Payout terms (17 June 2024) and documentation say it pays "irrespective of whether the funds are actually received by Adyen from the Acquirer or Scheme Owner", capped at 30 days, then reversed. Stripe documents paying ahead only for Instant Payouts. No chapter may say this is "not public" for both. A provider's payout runs on the provider's own schedule, not necessarily "after" network settlement.
19. **Visa's retry limit** is confirmed from the Visa Core Rules of 24 October 2026 (Table 7-2): 20 reattempts in 30 days. Adyen's documentation page still says 15; cite Visa for the rule and note the discrepancy. Mastercard's limit is known only from secondary sources.
20. **The Silent Webhook is Monday 14 September 2026**: the certificate expired shortly after midnight, events failed for 9 hours, and members were turned away on Monday morning. Earlier wording ("Saturday night", "Sunday morning") was wrong for that date; no page may name Saturday or Sunday for it.
21. **Two errors in the billing brief.** The Adyen knowledge-hub article "Why Adyen acquired Orb" is dated 29 September 2026 on the page (the brief says 19 October). The sentence "We completed our acquisition of Orb on 1 July." is **not** on that page: do not quote it. The closing date (1 July 2026) is supported by Adyen's closing press release.
22. **No external URLs in the running text.** As in ORDERED, a fact is attributed in the sentence (who, what kind of document, year or "n.d., accessed November 2026") and its URL appears once, under Sources and Evidence. Chapters drafted with inline `[text](https://…)` links have them reduced to plain text in the review pass; every such URL must already be in the page's Sources section. Internal `[[permalink]]` links are unaffected.
23. **Larkfield's Stripe studios are legacy Custom connected accounts** (destination charges with `on_behalf_of`); Larkfield collected verification requirements through its own API forms from 2017 and moves to Stripe's embedded onboarding component under K6. No page describes them as Express accounts.
24. **Eight incidents, seven of them in 2026.** The Unexplained €0.4 Million is a year-end 2025 difference found in January 2026. Do not write "eight incidents in 2026".
25. **Publication dates are the dates displayed on the page**, not dates from page metadata. Where the two differ by a day (several Adyen knowledge-hub posts do), use the displayed date: for example GlobalStream 6 October 2025, "Partitioning an environment" 16 September 2025, Architect 23 August 2026, "Why billing and payments belong together" 29 September 2026. A post is cited with the same date on every page.
26. **How Larkfield books a refund after payout.** Chapter 35 owns it: the refund is booked against "owed to studio" when the studio's balance covers it (Studio Noord's balance on 18 March 2027 is €212.40, so Mila's €84.00 refund is covered); the clearing account "to recover from studio" is used only for the part a studio's balance cannot cover. Chapter 12's example must agree.
27. **Ruling 11 refined (from the verification of chapter 1).** (a) Stripe's acquirer disclosure names *acquirers*, not always banks: in Japan they are card companies (Sumitomo Mitsui Card, JCB), so write "named acquirers" or "named partner banks and card companies", not "partner banks" alone. The disclosure also covers Mexico (Stripe is both acquirer and payment facilitator) and the UAE (Stripe is an independent sales organization of Network International); a list that names the United States, Canada, Japan, India and Thailand must not be presented as complete. (b) **Adyen is not silent on where it acquires**: its documentation page "Supported currencies" (`docs.adyen.com/account/supported-currencies`, n.d., accessed November 2026) lists 14 countries and regions with an "Adyen acquiring connection" and says payments are processed "through one of our acquiring entities, for example Adyen Singapore". What Adyen does not publish is the legal entity that acquires in each country. No page may say Adyen "publishes no list per country"; say "names no acquiring entity per country". (c) Adyen's annual-report phrase about banking partners is a concentration-risk statement; reading it as a description of acquiring is the author's inference and is labeled so.
28. **When Larkfield's ledger is written.** Under K3 (chapter 15 owns it) ledger entries are written only from provider report lines, by the reconciliation job on each provider's report-ready signal. A webhook changes the payment record store, never the ledger. Chapter 12's description of the pre-Keel 2025 ledger (a nightly job posting when a payment became `paid`) is history; its description of the Keel design must agree with chapter 15.
29. **Ruling 12 scoped (from the verification of chapter 8).** "No endpoint that returns a payment" is true of Adyen's Checkout API for online payments; Adyen's Terminal API has a transaction status request for in-person payments. Say "for online payments" or "in the Checkout API" wherever the claim is made. Also: the reason for Adyen's `/sessions` design is on record (Adyen knowledge-hub post, 5 October 2021: API calls "reduced from three to only one"); only the reasons for result codes and asynchronous modifications are not.
30. **Documentation pages are cited "n.d., accessed November 2026".** `docs.adyen.com` and `docs.stripe.com` pages display no publication date; "last modified" stamps from `.md` front matter or page metadata are unreliable (one build stamp is shared by many pages, and a page stamped 2023 mentions 2026) and are not used as dates, in the text or in Sources. Dated material (blog posts, press releases, release notes, changelogs, filings) keeps its displayed date (ruling 25).
